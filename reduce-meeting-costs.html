How to Reduce Meeting Costs: 7 Strategies That Work

Last updated March 2026 · 7 min read · By the MeetingBurn Team

Here is a number that might make you uncomfortable. According to research from Fellow, meetings cost the average organization about $29,000 per employee per year. For a 50 person company, that is roughly $1.45 million going to meetings annually. Not all of it is wasted, but a significant chunk of it is.

The Wharton Center for Applied Research found that managers can reduce time spent in meetings by 25% simply by using meetings more appropriately, and effective management of remaining meetings can cut another 20%. That translates to real money. If you want to see what your specific numbers look like, plug them into our meeting cost calculator and check the savings simulator.

These seven strategies are drawn from published workplace research and from what we have observed in the data. None of them require expensive software or organizational overhauls. They require discipline and a willingness to change habits.

Strategy 01
Apply the "could this be an email" test to every meeting
Before sending a meeting invite, ask yourself honestly: does this require real time conversation, or am I scheduling a meeting because it feels easier than writing a clear message? Atlassian's research found that 80% of workers believe they would be more productive with fewer meetings. A large portion of recurring meetings exist purely out of habit. Status updates, FYI announcements, and information sharing almost always work better as written communication. Save meetings for decisions, debates, and brainstorms where real time interaction creates value.
Strategy 02
Default to 25 minutes instead of 30
Calendar tools default to 30 or 60 minute slots, so that is what people book. But Parkinson's Law applies: work expands to fill the time available. Switch your default meeting length to 25 minutes (or 50 for longer sessions). Flowtrace's 2025 data shows the median meeting has already dropped to 35 minutes as organizations push toward shorter sessions. That 5 minute buffer between meetings also gives people time to recover focus before the next one, which matters more than you might think. UC Irvine research shows it takes over 23 minutes to regain full focus after an interruption.
Strategy 03
Cut the invite list ruthlessly
Every person you add to a meeting multiplies the cost linearly. A meeting with 8 people costs exactly twice as much as the same meeting with 4 people. Yet Flowtrace's data shows 29% of recurring meetings pull in 7 or more participants. Ask yourself for each name on the invite: does this person need to speak, decide, or listen? If the answer is just "listen," send them the notes afterward instead. Our savings simulator shows exactly how much you save by removing 2 attendees.
Strategy 04
Require an agenda for every meeting with 3 or more people
This is possibly the highest impact change you can make. 60% of standalone meetings lack a documented agenda, according to Flowtrace. An agenda does not need to be elaborate. Three bullet points explaining what will be discussed and what outcome is expected is enough. It takes 2 minutes to write and can save 15 minutes of wandering discussion. We have a free agenda template designed around the practices that research links to productive meetings.
Strategy 05
Institute one or two meeting free days per week
Several major companies have adopted this approach. Shopify famously purged most recurring meetings from their calendars in 2023. The principle is simple: designate certain days where no internal meetings are scheduled, giving people unbroken time for focused work. ActivTrak's 2026 data shows the average focused work session is down to just 13 minutes. Protected meeting free blocks directly address this fragmentation problem.
Strategy 06
Replace recurring meetings with async updates
Weekly status meetings are one of the largest sources of meeting cost in most organizations. Try replacing them with a structured async format: each team member posts a short written update (what they did, what they plan to do, any blockers) in a shared channel. Reserve synchronous meeting time for when there are actual decisions to make or problems to solve. Tools like Loom, Slack, and Notion make this straightforward.
Strategy 07
Make meeting cost visible
This is what our tool was built for. When people can see that their weekly team sync costs $400 per session and $20,800 per year, they start thinking differently about whether every person on the invite needs to be there, and whether the meeting needs to run the full 60 minutes. Shopify built an internal meeting cost calculator for exactly this reason, and it changed their meeting culture. You can do the same thing for free with our meeting cost calculator. Share it with your team before the next meeting and let the numbers speak for themselves.

The Math Behind These Savings

Let's run a concrete example. Say your company has a weekly all hands with 12 people, average salary of $90,000, lasting 60 minutes with benefits overhead included. That single meeting costs roughly $877 per session, which adds up to $45,600 per year.

Now apply three of the strategies above. Cut the invite list from 12 to 8 people. Shorten it from 60 to 45 minutes. Move to twice monthly instead of weekly. The cost drops to roughly $8,775 per year. You just saved $36,825 from one meeting.

Multiply that across every recurring meeting in your organization and the numbers become staggering. Use our calculator to run the specific numbers for your team.

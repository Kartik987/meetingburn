7 Ways to Cut Meeting Costs Without Losing Productivity

Last updated March 2026 · 8 min read · By the MeetingBurn team

Fellow's 2024 workplace report estimates meeting time costs organizations roughly $29,000 per employee per year. That number sounds abstract until you multiply it by your headcount. For a 50 person company, that is nearly $1.5 million in salary time going to meetings annually. Not all of that is wasted, but the research consistently shows a significant portion of it is.

The good news: you do not need to eliminate meetings. You need to eliminate the unnecessary ones and make the necessary ones shorter. Here are seven strategies, ordered from easiest to implement to most impactful at scale. Every strategy includes the math so you can see the financial impact for your own team using our meeting cost calculator.

1 Set 25 minute defaults instead of 30

This takes 60 seconds to change in your calendar settings and it compounds immediately. A team of 10 people having 8 meetings per week saves 40 minutes of collective time per week just from the 5 minute trim. That is over 34 hours per year. At an average loaded salary of $110,000, those recovered hours are worth roughly $1,800 annually. Multiply that by every team in your organization.

2 Apply the "could this be an email?" test

Atlassian found that 55% of remote workers believe most of their meetings could have been an email. Before scheduling any meeting, ask yourself: am I sharing information (send an email) or gathering input on a document (use comments)? Meetings should be reserved for live discussion, real time decision making, and sensitive conversations. Status updates almost never need a meeting.

3 Remove 2 people from every meeting

This sounds blunt, but the math is compelling. Meeting cost is linear with attendees. A 30 minute meeting with 8 people at $90,000 average salary costs about $282. Drop it to 6 people and it costs $212. That is $70 saved per meeting. Do that across 4 meetings per week and you save $14,500 per year. More importantly, those 2 people get their 30 minutes back for actual work. Send them the meeting minutes afterward so they stay informed.

4 Require written agendas for all meetings

Flowtrace data shows 60% of standalone meetings lack a written agenda. Those meetings are the ones people rate as unproductive. Implementing a policy that every meeting invitation must include an agenda does two things: it improves the quality of meetings that happen, and it prevents some meetings from happening at all because the organizer realizes they do not have enough to fill an agenda.

5 Introduce one no meeting day per week

Shopify made headlines by aggressively cutting meetings, including implementing meeting free days. The concept is simple: designate one day (many teams choose Wednesday or Friday) where no meetings are scheduled. This gives everyone a guaranteed block of uninterrupted focus time. Research from ActivTrak shows the average focused session is just 13 minutes. A full meeting free day can reset that pattern and give people space for deep work.

6 Replace recurring meetings with async updates

Weekly status meetings are among the most expensive and least productive meetings in most organizations. If each person spends 2 minutes giving an update to a room of 8, that is a 16 minute meeting costing around $150. A Slack thread or Loom video achieves the same information sharing at zero meeting cost, and people can consume the updates when it suits their schedule rather than interrupting their workflow.

7 Make meeting costs visible

This is what our meeting cost calculator exists for. When people see a real dollar amount ticking up during a meeting, it changes behavior. Shopify built an internal meeting cost calculator for exactly this reason and reported that it shifted their meeting culture significantly. Visibility creates accountability. Teams that track meeting costs consistently report making more intentional decisions about when and how they meet.

Where to start: Pick strategies 1 and 4 first. Changing calendar defaults to 25 minutes takes one minute. Requiring agendas takes one email to your team. Combined, they will save more time and money than you expect, with almost no friction.

Meeting Minutes Template That Focuses on What Matters

Last updated March 2026 · 5 min read · By the MeetingBurn team

Most meeting minutes try to capture everything that was said. That is a mistake. Nobody reads a transcript. What people actually need after a meeting is a short record of three things: what was decided, what needs to happen next, and who is responsible for each action. Everything else is noise.

We built this template around that principle. It takes about 2 minutes to fill out during or immediately after a meeting. It is deliberately short because the goal is not documentation for its own sake. The goal is making sure things actually get done.

The Template

Meeting Title[Same as the agenda title]
Date[Date and actual duration]
Attendees[Who was actually present]
Key Decisions[What did we decide? Be specific.]
Action 1[Task] → [Owner] → [Due date]
Action 2[Task] → [Owner] → [Due date]
Action 3[Task] → [Owner] → [Due date]
Open Questions[Unresolved items for next time]
Next Meeting[Date, or "not needed"]

Why This Format Works

Atlassian's research found that 54% of workers leave meetings without a clear idea of what to do next. Think about the cost of that. You just spent 30 minutes with 6 people (roughly $160 in loaded salary using our meeting cost calculator) and more than half the room walks out confused about next steps. That is not a meeting problem. That is a documentation problem.

The "Key Decisions" field is arguably the most valuable part. Meetings exist to make decisions. If no decision was made, you need to write that down too, because it means the meeting failed at its primary purpose. Over time, reviewing these fields shows you which meetings consistently produce decisions and which ones are just discussion loops going nowhere.

Action items need three components or they don't work: the task itself, the person responsible, and a due date. "We should look into this" is not an action item. "Sarah will research vendor pricing and share findings by Friday" is. Research on task completion consistently shows that specificity and ownership are the strongest predictors of follow through.

Practical approach: Send the completed minutes within 15 minutes of the meeting ending. The longer you wait, the less accurate your notes become and the less urgency people feel about the action items. A short, immediate summary beats a perfect summary sent three days later every time.

Minutes vs. Transcription

Tools like Otter.ai can transcribe entire meetings word for word. That is useful for reference, but it is not the same as meeting minutes. A transcript is raw data. Minutes are the processed insight. Someone still needs to extract the decisions, identify the action items, and assign owners. Our template gives you the structure for that extraction step.

If your meetings regularly end without clear decisions or action items, the problem is likely upstream. Take a look at our meeting agenda template. Meetings that start with a defined purpose and desired outcome are far more likely to end with concrete results worth documenting.

Meeting Cost Per Employee: The Numbers by Salary Level

Last updated March 2026 · 5 min read · By the MeetingBurn team

When Fellow's 2024 research says meetings cost roughly $29,000 per employee per year, that number assumes a particular salary level and meeting load. But every organization is different. A software engineering team at $150,000 average salary burns through meeting budgets at a completely different rate than an operations team at $55,000. Here is the actual math at every salary level.

Cost Breakdown by Annual Salary

This table assumes 11.3 hours of meetings per week (the average from Fellow's research), with a 30% benefits overhead applied. All figures represent loaded cost, meaning what the organization actually pays including benefits, employer taxes, and overhead.

The methodology behind these numbers is straightforward: annual salary times 1.30 (benefits overhead), divided by 2,080 working hours per year, gives you the loaded hourly rate. Multiply that by 11.3 meeting hours per week and 52 weeks per year for the annual figure. What Jumps Out

At $150,000 salary, which is common for senior engineers and product managers, each employee's meeting time costs over $55,000 per year. That is more than many junior roles earn in total. Executives at $250,000 approach $92,000 in annual meeting costs per person. When three executives sit in a weekly hour long meeting together, the annual loaded cost of that single recurring meeting is over $23,000.

The numbers get uncomfortable fast when you consider that Atlassian found only 11% of meetings are rated "highly productive." If even half of the remaining meetings are partially wasted, an organization with 100 employees at an $85,000 average salary is losing somewhere in the range of $1 million to $1.5 million annually on unproductive meeting time.

The focus recovery multiplier: These figures do not include the 23 minutes and 15 seconds it takes to regain deep focus after each meeting (UC Irvine research). If you add that recovery time to every meeting, the true cost increases by roughly 40 to 75% depending on meeting length. A 30 minute meeting actually consumes 53 minutes of productive capacity. What You Can Do With This Information

Knowing the per employee cost is the starting point for making the business case to leadership. When you can say "our 8 person engineering team spends $441,000 per year in meeting time, and research suggests at least 30% of that is recoverable," you have a concrete financial argument for changing meeting culture. Our guide to reducing meeting costs walks through seven specific strategies with estimated savings for each one.

For teams looking to immediately improve meeting quality, the simplest first step is requiring a written meeting agenda for every meeting with three or more people. Flowtrace data shows this single practice correlates strongly with higher productivity ratings. It also naturally reduces unnecessary meetings because people realize they do not have enough material to justify the invite.

How Much Do Meetings Cost Per Employee? The Real Numbers

Last updated March 2026 · 6 min read · By the MeetingBurn Team

If someone asked you to name your company's third or fourth largest expense, meetings probably would not come to mind. But once you do the math, the numbers are hard to ignore.

~$29,000
Estimated annual meeting cost per employee (salary plus lost productivity)
Fellow, 2024 Workplace Report

That figure from Fellow's 2024 research accounts for both the direct salary cost of time spent in meetings and the indirect productivity loss from context switching and recovery time. Cirrus Insight's 2026 analysis arrived at a similar number. Bloomberg has cited aggregate estimates reaching $375 billion annually across all U.S. businesses.

But how exactly do you get to $29,000? Let's break it down.

The Calculation

Fellow's data puts the average employee at 11.3 hours per week in meetings. That is roughly 588 hours per year (11.3 multiplied by 52 weeks). For an employee earning $85,000 per year, the hourly rate comes to about $40.87. Add 30% for benefits overhead (a conservative estimate based on Bureau of Labor Statistics data) and the loaded rate becomes $53.13 per hour.

588 hours multiplied by $53.13 gives you $31,240 in direct salary cost spent on meetings per employee per year. That number alone is striking. But it does not include the hidden costs.

The Hidden Costs Most People Miss

Focus recovery time

Research by Gloria Mark at the University of California, Irvine found it takes 23 minutes and 15 seconds on average to fully refocus after an interruption. Every meeting creates this recovery window for every attendee. If an employee has 4 meetings per day, that is roughly 93 minutes of daily recovery time. Over a year, that adds up to over 400 hours of diminished productivity that never shows up in any meeting calendar.

Preparation time

Most meetings require some preparation, even if it is just reviewing the agenda or pulling up relevant documents. Research estimates 15 to 30 minutes of prep per meeting on average. For an employee attending 2 to 3 meetings per day, that is another 30 to 90 minutes of meeting related work that falls outside the meeting itself.

The context switching tax

ActivTrak's 2026 analysis of 443 million work hours found that the average focused session has shrunk to just 13 minutes and 7 seconds. Meetings are a primary driver of this fragmentation. Each time someone shifts from focused work to a meeting and back again, there is a cognitive cost. It is invisible but cumulative.

What This Means for Your Team

For a team of 10, meeting costs approach $300,000 per year. For 50 people, you are looking at roughly $1.5 million. These are not theoretical numbers. They are the mathematical result of converting salary time into meeting time at the rates your people are actually paid.

The question is not whether to eliminate meetings entirely. Many meetings produce real value. The question is whether every meeting on your calendar is producing value proportional to its cost.

Meeting Cost Per Employee: The Full Annual Breakdown

Fellow's 2024 Workplace Report estimates meetings cost organizations roughly $29,000 per employee per year. The exact number depends heavily on salary levels. Here is what the math actually looks like at different pay tiers.

That $29,000 figure is the aggregate average. The reality for your specific team is probably different, and potentially much higher. Meeting cost per employee scales roughly linearly with salary, so senior employees and executives cost significantly more in meeting time than entry level staff. The breakdown below shows what the numbers actually look like at four representative salary tiers.

How the Calculation Works

Here is the math step by step for a mid career employee earning $95,000 per year.

Step 1. Base hourly rate
$95,000 / 2,080 hours = $45.67/hour

Step 2. Loaded hourly rate (with 30% benefits overhead)
$45.67 × 1.30 = $59.37/hour

Step 3. Weekly meeting cost (11.3 hrs avg per Fellow)
$59.37 × 11.3 = $670.89/week

Step 4. Annual meeting cost
$670.89 × 52 weeks = $34,886/year

What This Means for Team Budgets

Individual meeting cost is only part of the picture. Teams typically scale the cost multiplicatively because meetings involve multiple people simultaneously. Consider a 10 person product team with an average salary of $110,000.

Per employee, annual meeting cost at $110,000 salary runs approximately $40,408. Multiplied across the full team: $404,080 per year. Add a few executive level stakeholders who routinely attend and the number climbs quickly. For many early stage startups, the annual meeting bill exceeds the cost of their entire software stack.

This is why reducing meeting costs is such leverage. A 20% reduction in meeting time (which is conservative based on Wharton research) for this team saves $80,816 annually. That is roughly one additional engineer hire, funded entirely by reclaiming time from meetings.

Worth knowing: These are conservative numbers. They include 30% benefits overhead but exclude context switching cost, preparation time, and the focus recovery tax (23 minutes 15 seconds of reduced productivity after every interruption, per UC Irvine research). Real total cost is typically 1.5x to 2x the pure salary time.

The Hidden Costs Most Calculations Miss

Focus recovery tax. UC Irvine's research on interrupted work found that it takes 23 minutes and 15 seconds on average to fully return to a task after an interruption. Every meeting triggers this for every attendee. If an employee has 4 meetings per day, that is 93 minutes of additional productivity loss per person per day that never shows up in any meeting calendar.

Preparation time. Most meetings require 10 to 30 minutes of preparation. Reviewing the agenda, pulling up relevant documents, reading pre read materials. For an employee with 12 meetings per week, preparation time alone adds 2 to 6 hours of weekly cost that is rarely tracked.

Follow up time. Meetings generate action items, follow up emails, and post meeting calls. These are productive in the sense that they move work forward, but they represent additional time spent because the meeting happened. A rough estimate is 15 to 20% of meeting time in follow up work.

When you add these up, Fellow's $29,000 per employee figure is likely conservative. Honest accounting puts total annual meeting related cost per knowledge worker closer to $40,000 to $55,000, depending on salary and seniority.

Practical use: You probably cannot reduce all of these costs, but naming them helps in conversations with finance teams who want to understand why improvements in meeting culture are worth investment. Use the conservative number ($29K) for public conversations and the fuller number privately to evaluate actual ROI.

How Meeting Cost Varies by Industry

Meeting patterns differ significantly across industries. Software and technology companies tend to have more meetings per week than traditional industries but also shorter meetings on average. Consulting firms typically have the highest hourly meeting cost because of the senior salary profile. Manufacturing and production industries generally have the lowest per employee meeting cost, though those numbers can spike for management and operations roles.

Asana's 2024 global report found substantial geographic variation as well. Workers in France report losing roughly 9.1 hours per week to unproductive meetings, Germany 8.8, Japan 8.3, the U.S. 5.2, and the U.K. 4.1. Multiply these hours by local salary norms and the picture shifts considerably. A French knowledge worker may have a lower dollar salary but higher meeting cost ratio than a U.S. counterpart.

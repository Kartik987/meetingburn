The Shrinking Focus Session

How long can your team actually focus without interruption? Not long, according to recent data. ActivTrak analyzed over 443 million hours of work behavior across 1,111 companies for their 2026 State of the Workplace report and found the average focused session has fallen to just 13 minutes and 7 seconds. That's a 9% decline since 2023. Meetings and collaboration tools are the primary culprits fragmenting the modern workday into ever smaller blocks.
ActivTrak, "2026 State of the Workplace," 163,638 employees
⏱

11.3 Hours Per Week in Meetings

The typical employee now gives up roughly 11.3 hours every week to meetings, about 28% of a standard work week. Over a quarter of your team's paid time goes to sitting in rooms, physical or virtual, talking. Atlassian's survey makes the frustration clear: 80% of workers say they'd be more productive with fewer meetings, and 68% report they don't get enough uninterrupted time for focused work.
Fellow, 2024; Atlassian Work Efficiency Survey
📊

Shorter, Smaller Meetings Win

There's good news. Organizations are figuring this out. Flowtrace analyzed 1.3 million meetings in 2025 and found the median duration has dropped to 35 minutes. Just 12% run beyond an hour now, and 64% include six or fewer people. Teams that keep meetings short, small, and agenda driven consistently report better outcomes than those running long unfocused sessions with large invite lists.
Flowtrace, "State of Meetings Report 2025"
🌍

Meeting Waste Varies by Country

Meeting culture is not universal. Asana's 2024 global survey revealed striking differences: workers in France reported losing roughly 9.1 hours per week to unproductive meetings, Germany 8.8, Japan 8.3, the U.S. 5.2, and the U.K. 4.1. These numbers reflect deeply embedded organizational habits that vary significantly from one country's work culture to another.
Asana, "Anatomy of Work" Global Report, 2024

Transparent Methodology

How We Calculate Meeting Cost

Every number in this tool comes from your inputs and standard pay formulas. No hidden variables, no inflated figures. Here's the exact process, step by step.

Calculation Method

1
Hourly Rate: Annual salary divided by 2,080 hours per year (52 weeks times 40 hours). This is the standard used by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and most payroll systems. $85,000 / 2,080 = $40.87/hr
2
True Cost to Employer: Hourly rate multiplied by 1.30 for employer costs including health insurance, payroll taxes, retirement, and overhead. BLS data shows benefits average 29 to 32% of total compensation. $40.87 x 1.30 = $53.13/hr
3
Per Minute Rate: Cost-to-employer hourly rate divided by 60, multiplied by number of attendees. ($53.13 / 60) x 6 = $5.31/min
4
Session Cost: Per minute rate multiplied by duration. $5.31 x 30 = $159.38
5
Cost of Refocusing: Per second rate multiplied by 1,395 seconds (23 min 15 sec) for all attendees, the time research says it takes to get back to deep work. Based on UC Irvine research by Mark, Gudith, and Klocke.
6
Projections: Session cost multiplied by frequency. Daily = 260 per year, Weekly = 52, Twice monthly = 26, Monthly = 12, Once = 1. Carbon estimate uses approximately 0.04 kg CO₂ per participant hour.

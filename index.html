The Shrinking Focus Session

How long can your team actually focus without interruption? Not long, according to recent data. ActivTrak analyzed over 443 million hours of work behavior across 1,111 companies for their 2026 State of the Workplace report and found the average focused session has fallen to just 13 minutes and 7 seconds. That represents a 9% decline since 2023. Meetings and collaboration tools are the primary culprits fragmenting the modern workday into ever smaller blocks.
ActivTrak, "2026 State of the Workplace," 163,638 employees across 1,111 companies
⏱

11.3 Hours Per Week in Meetings

The typical employee now gives up roughly 11.3 hours every week to meetings, which works out to about 28% of a standard work week. Think about that for a moment: over a quarter of your team's paid time goes to sitting in rooms (physical or virtual) talking. Atlassian's survey data makes the frustration clear. 80% of workers say they would be more productive with fewer meetings, and 68% report that they simply do not get enough uninterrupted time for focused work during the day.
Fellow Workplace Report, 2024; Atlassian Work Efficiency Survey
📊

Shorter, Smaller Meetings Work Better

There is good news. Organizations are starting to figure this out. Flowtrace looked at 1.3 million meetings in 2025 and found the median duration has dropped to 35 minutes. Just 12% of meetings run beyond an hour now, and 64% include six or fewer people. The pattern is clear: teams that keep meetings short, small, and focused around a written agenda consistently report better outcomes than those running long, unfocused sessions with large invite lists.
Flowtrace, "State of Meetings Report 2025," 1.3M meetings analyzed
🌍

Meeting Waste Varies by Country

Meeting culture is not universal. Asana's 2024 global survey revealed striking differences across countries. Workers in France reported losing roughly 9.1 hours per week to unproductive meetings, with Germany close behind at 8.8 and Japan at 8.3. U.S. workers reported 5.2 hours of weekly waste, while the U.K. came in lowest at 4.1. These aren't small numbers. They point to deeply embedded organizational habits that vary from one country's work culture to another.
Asana, "Anatomy of Work" Global Report, 2024
Transparent Methodology

How We Calculate Meeting Cost

We believe in showing our work. Every number the calculator produces comes from your inputs and standard compensation formulas. No hidden variables, no inflated figures. Here is the exact process, step by step.

Calculation Method

1
Hourly Rate: Annual salary divided by 2,080 hours per year (52 weeks times 40 hours). This is the standard used by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and most payroll systems. $85,000 / 2,080 = $40.87/hr
2
Loaded Cost: Hourly rate multiplied by 1.30 for employer costs including health insurance, FICA, retirement contributions, and overhead. BLS data shows employer benefits average 29 to 32% of total compensation. $40.87 x 1.30 = $53.13/hr
3
Per Minute Rate: Loaded hourly rate divided by 60, multiplied by number of attendees. ($53.13 / 60) x 6 = $5.31/min
4
Session Cost: Per minute rate multiplied by duration. $5.31 x 30 = $159.38
5
Focus Recovery Tax: Per second rate multiplied by 1,395 seconds (23 min 15 sec) for all attendees. Based on UC Irvine research by Mark, Gudith, and Klocke.
6
Projections: Session cost multiplied by frequency. Daily = 260 per year, Weekly = 52, Twice monthly = 26, Monthly = 12, Once = 1. Carbon estimate uses approximately 0.04 kg CO₂ per participant hour.
